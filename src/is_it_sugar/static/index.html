        Common choices

        Small amounts

        When a food's measured fructose load is under this limit, it shows Caution instead of Avoid. Under 0.5 g it shows OK. Fructose load is fructose + half of sucrose + sorbitol, per 100 g. Set 0 to flag any amount.

        g per 100 g

        Only measured amounts count. In an ingredient list, each measured ingredient counts at its full amount per 100 g, the most it could add. A listed sugar or an AI estimate keeps the food at Avoid.

        How matching works

        • An ingredient matches when its name contains what you added: “onion” covers “onion powder”.
        • A whole food is allowed only by its own name: “broccoli” covers “Broccoli, raw”, but “tomato” doesn't cover “tomato soup”.
        • Sugars from the BU table are allowed only by name: “corn” never allows “corn syrup”. Avoiding “corn” does flag “corn syrup”.
        • If something is on both lists, avoiding wins.
        • Your lists and recent checks are saved in this browser, on this device.
        Sugars are judged using the Boston University HFI sugar table, foods are looked up in USDA FoodData Central, packaged foods also in Open Food Facts (ODbL), and, for whole foods, the Australian Food Composition Database (Food Standards Australia New Zealand, Release 3, CC BY 4.0) and the ANSES-CIQUAL French food composition table (2020, Licence Ouverte), and anything else is classified by an AI model, which can make mistakes. Always read the label, and check with your doctor or dietitian before relying on a result.
